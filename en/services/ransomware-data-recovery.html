Ransomware Data Recovery & Decryption in Jeddah

A ransomware attack is one of the most devastating crises a business can face. Malicious actors infiltrate your network, encrypt your critical databases, accounting files, and documents, and demand a massive cryptocurrency payment for the decryption key. Paying the ransom is highly risky and often leads to further extortion. Al-Fares Center provides specialized ransomware data recovery in Jeddah. We utilize advanced reverse-engineering, cryptographic analysis, and deep forensic data carving to restore access to your vital systems without engaging with cybercriminals.

Signs of a Ransomware Infection

Ransomware strikes suddenly. If you notice any of the following signs, disconnect your computer from the network and Wi-Fi immediately to prevent the virus from spreading to servers and backups:

  • Changed File Extensions: All your files suddenly have strange extensions added to them (e.g., .lockbit, .phobos, .mkos, .stop, .djvu, .encrypted).
  • Ransom Note Files: You find text or HTML files named "!README_RESTORE_FILES.txt" or "HOW_TO_DECRYPT.txt" placed in every folder on your drive.
  • Files Will Not Open: When you try to open a PDF, Word document, or image, the application displays an error saying the file is corrupted or in an unsupported format.
  • Desktop Wallpaper Changed: The hackers have forcibly changed your desktop background to a menacing message detailing their demands and a countdown timer.
  • High CPU / Disk Usage: The computer suddenly becomes incredibly slow as the malicious software works aggressively in the background to encrypt thousands of files.

Our specialized recovery approach

Handling a ransomware case requires strict isolation protocols to prevent reinfection and advanced forensic techniques to bypass the encryption:

  1. Containment & Cloning: We never attempt recovery on the infected live machine. We immediately clone the compromised drives at the sector level. All recovery attempts are performed on these isolated lab copies within a secure sandbox environment.
  2. Malware Identification: We analyze the encryption signature, file extensions, and ransom note to precisely identify the ransomware family and variant (e.g., STOP/Djvu, Phobos, Dharma, LockBit).
  3. Cryptographic Analysis: We cross-reference the identified variant against global cybersecurity databases to determine if a master decryption key has been leaked or if an offline key decryptor exists for that specific strain.
  4. Volume Shadow Copy & Unallocated Space Recovery: Ransomware typically creates an encrypted copy of a file and deletes the original. We utilize deep hex-level data carving to scan the unallocated space and Volume Shadow Copies (VSS) for remnants of the original, unencrypted files before they are permanently overwritten.
  5. Database Repair: If an SQL or ERP database file was partially encrypted, we perform structural hex-editing to rebuild the database headers and extract the unencrypted tables and records.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

I see a text file demanding money. Should I pay the hackers?

We strongly advise against paying. You are dealing with criminals; there is zero guarantee they will send the decryption key, and paying them often marks you as a target for future attacks. Allow our experts to analyze the infection first to determine if alternative decryption is possible.

Can I just use anti-virus software to fix my files?

No. Anti-virus software will detect and remove the malicious ransomware executable, which stops the ongoing encryption process. However, it cannot undo the encryption or restore your altered files. Decryption requires specific cryptographic keys or shadow copy restoration.

Can you decrypt all types of ransomware?

The possibility of decryption depends heavily on the specific ransomware variant and whether it uses an "offline" or "online" encryption key. While some older or flawed variants (like certain STOP/Djvu IDs) have known decryptors, advanced enterprise ransomware (like LockBit) requires extensive forensic data carving to bypass.

Is it safe to connect my external backup drive now?

Absolutely not. If the ransomware is still active in your computer's memory, it will immediately detect the external drive and encrypt your backup files as well. Turn off the computer immediately.
