RAID & NAS Server Data Recovery in Jeddah

Corporate servers and Network Attached Storage (NAS) systems form the digital backbone of any business. When a RAID array collapses—whether due to multiple drive failures, power surges, or an accidental rebuild—the resulting operational downtime is catastrophic. Al-Fares Center is the leading authority in enterprise RAID data recovery in Jeddah. Our engineers possess the deep architectural knowledge required to virtually reconstruct complex arrays (RAID 0, 1, 5, 6, 10, and 50) and recover critical databases, virtual machines, and company shares with minimal downtime.

Common Causes of RAID Array Failures

RAID systems are designed for redundancy, but they are not immune to failure. We commonly handle cases involving:

  • Multiple Simultaneous Drive Failures: While RAID 5 can survive one failed drive, a sudden power surge or thermal issue can cause two or more drives to fail simultaneously, collapsing the entire array.
  • Failed RAID Rebuilds: When a degraded array attempts to rebuild using a new drive, the intense strain can cause a second aging drive to fail mid-process, corrupting the parity distribution.
  • RAID Controller Failure: The hardware RAID controller card burns out or loses its configuration, leaving the raw drives perfectly healthy but completely inaccessible because the stripe maps are lost.
  • NAS Firmware Corruption: Network Attached Storage devices (like QNAP, Synology, or WD MyCloud) can suffer from corrupted Linux-based operating systems, making the network shares suddenly vanish.
  • Accidental Deletion or Formatting: An IT administrator accidentally initializes the wrong array, or malicious actors delete vital enterprise volumes.

Our specialized recovery approach

RAID recovery requires highly advanced mathematics and specialized software; a single mistake in stripe calculation can scramble the data permanently:

  1. Independent Drive Imaging: We never work directly on the live server hardware. We safely remove each hard drive and create exact, sector-by-sector images using hardware write-blockers to preserve the original state.
  2. Physical Repairs if Necessary: If any individual drives have suffered mechanical head crashes or PCB damage, we repair them in our Class 100 clean room to ensure we have maximum data available for the rebuild.
  3. Array Parameter Analysis: Our engineers do not need the original RAID controller. Using advanced Hex-editors, we analyze the raw data to manually determine the block size, disk order, parity rotation algorithm, and start offsets.
  4. Virtual Array Reconstruction: Utilizing specialized forensic software, we build a virtual instance of the RAID array using our calculated parameters, allowing us to safely interpret the distributed data as a single logical volume.
  5. Data Extraction & Verification: Once the volume is reconstructed, we extract the virtual machines (VMDK/VHD), SQL databases, and file servers, meticulously verifying their structural integrity before delivering them to the client.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Should I try to rebuild the RAID array myself?

No. If a RAID array fails or degrades, attempting a rebuild without identifying the exact cause of failure often overwrites the distributed parity data, destroying the volume permanently. Power off the server immediately to preserve the data integrity.

We lost the RAID controller configuration. Can the data be recovered?

Yes. Our engineers do not rely on the original RAID controller. We image each drive individually and use specialized hex-editing and algorithms to manually calculate the block size, parity rotation, and stripe order to virtually reconstruct the array.

How long does RAID recovery usually take?

RAID recovery duration depends on the total number of drives, their physical condition, and array capacity. We offer emergency priority services for enterprise clients experiencing critical downtime to expedite the recovery process.

Do you recover from virtual machines hosted on RAID servers?

Absolutely. We routinely recover VHD, VHDX, and VMDK virtual machine files hosted on VMware or Hyper-V environments from collapsed RAID 5 or RAID 6 arrays.
