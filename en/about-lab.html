Our Core Technology

Operating a professional data recovery lab requires immense investment in specialized laboratory infrastructure. Our facility is built upon three core technological pillars:

  • Certified Class 100 Clean Room: Traditional repair environments are saturated with microscopic dust particles that will permanently destroy exposed magnetic platters. Our certified Class 100 Clean Room guarantees a pristine, particle-free environment, which is an absolute necessity for safely performing mechanical surgeries, such as magnetic head replacements and platter swaps.
  • ACE Lab PC-3000 Systems: Standard software is entirely ineffective for hardware failures. We utilize the global gold standard in data extraction: the PC-3000 systems. These are considered to be the most cutting-edge solutions for diagnosis, repair, and data recovery from damaged storage devices. These advanced tools allow us to bypass BIOS restrictions, communicate directly with the drive, and repair corrupted firmware microcode to extract data from severely degraded media.
  • Advanced Micro-Soldering Stations: For modern storage devices with soldered NAND chips and complex logic boards, standard extraction methods fail. We utilize high-precision micro-soldering stations and tools like the Spider Board Adapter to perform intricate chip-off procedures and bypass burnt electrical components.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

1. Why shouldn't I try software recovery myself?
Using standard recovery software on a physically failing drive forces the damaged heads to continuously attempt to read degraded sectors. This constant mechanical stress will rapidly exacerbate the damage, potentially scoring the magnetic platters and rendering clean room data recovery permanently impossible.

2. What makes Al-Fares different from a regular computer repair shop?
Standard computer repair shops focus on replacing modular parts like screens and batteries to make the device functional again. They lack the Class 100 clean rooms, PC-3000 hardware suites, and micro-soldering expertise required to extract data from dead logic boards and crashed mechanical drives.
